Choosing a Diamond Shape

Shape is the first thing people notice about a diamond and the most personal choice you'll make. Here's what each of the ten common shapes looks like, how they differ in price and sparkle, and how to land on the right one — no sales pitch.

~6 min read · Updated July 2026

"Shape" and "cut" get used interchangeably, but they're different things. Shape is the outline of the stone — round, oval, pear, and so on. Cut is how well the stone's facets are proportioned to return light (that's one of the 4Cs). This guide is about shape: the look. Once you've picked a shape you love, our 4Cs guide covers how to judge the cut quality within it.

The two families: brilliant vs. step cuts

Almost every shape falls into one of two lighting styles, and knowing which is which tells you most of what to expect:

  • Brilliant cuts have triangular and kite-shaped facets designed to maximize sparkle and hide flaws. Round, oval, cushion, princess, pear, radiant, marquise, and heart are all brilliant-style. They twinkle, and they're forgiving of slightly lower clarity.
  • Step cuts have long, straight facets that run parallel to the edges, creating a clear "hall of mirrors" look instead of fiery sparkle. Emerald and Asscher are the step cuts. They're elegant and understated — but because you can see straight into them, inclusions and lower color show more easily.

A quick tour of the ten shapes

Round

The classic, and by far the most popular. Decades of engineering have optimized the round brilliant for maximum sparkle, so it outsparkles everything else and hides color and inclusions well. The trade-off: rounds cost the most per carat, both because of demand and because more of the rough crystal is polished away to make one.

Oval

A round's sparkle in an elongated outline. Ovals look larger than a round of the same carat weight because the shape spreads the surface out, and the elongation can flatter the finger. Watch for the "bow-tie" — a shadow across the center that varies stone to stone; a well-cut oval minimizes it.

Cushion

A soft, pillow-shaped square with rounded corners. Romantic and slightly vintage, with lots of sparkle and often a lower price than round or oval. Cushions come in squarer and more rectangular variants, so it's worth deciding which proportion you prefer.

Princess

The most popular square shape — sharp corners, a modern look, and strong sparkle. Because it's cut from the rough efficiently, you often get more carat for your money than a round. The pointed corners can chip, so a setting that protects them is a good idea.

Emerald

A rectangular step cut with cropped corners and that elegant hall-of-mirrors effect. Understated rather than sparkly, and it tends to look larger for its weight. Because the open facets reveal everything, spend a little more on clarity and color here than you would on a brilliant shape.

Pear

A teardrop — a round on one end tapering to a point on the other. Distinctive and elongating on the hand, with brilliant-cut sparkle. Like ovals, it can show a bow-tie, and symmetry matters a lot: the point should line up dead-center with the rounded end.

Radiant

A rectangular or square shape with cropped corners and full brilliant faceting — essentially the sparkle of a round in a bolder, more geometric outline. A great middle ground if you like the emerald's shape but want more fire, and it hides color and inclusions well.

Marquise

A long, pointed oval ("boat-shaped"). It has the largest surface area per carat of any shape, so it wears big and dramatic. The points are delicate and the bow-tie effect is common, so cut quality and a protective setting both matter.

Asscher

A square step cut — think of it as a square emerald with more dramatic, concentric facets. Vintage, architectural, and elegant. Same rule as the emerald: because it's so open, favor higher clarity and color.

Heart

The most romantic and the most demanding to cut well. Symmetry is everything — both lobes should match and the cleft should be crisp. Hearts read best at one carat and up, where the shape is easy to see.

Which shapes save you money?

Shape has a real effect on price, separate from the 4Cs. Round is the priciest per carat. Nearly every other shape — often called "fancy shapes" — costs less for the same carat, color, and clarity, sometimes meaningfully so. Fancy shapes also tend to look larger than a round of equal weight because their outlines spread wider.

The value play: if budget is tight and you don't have your heart set on a round, an oval, cushion, pear, or radiant can give you a noticeably bigger-looking stone for the same money — while still sparkling like a round.

A few practical tips

  • For step cuts (emerald, Asscher), buy clarity and color up a notch. Their open facets hide nothing, so a flaw that vanishes in a round will be visible here.
  • For elongated brilliants (oval, pear, marquise), check the bow-tie. Always look at the actual stone or a video — the shadow varies wildly between stones of identical specs.
  • Protect the points. Pear, marquise, princess, and heart all have vulnerable tips; a setting with prongs or a bezel over the point helps.
  • Consider the finger. Elongated shapes (oval, pear, marquise, emerald) tend to make fingers look longer and slimmer.
