Just Under a Carat: The Size Nobody Can See, for Less

A 1.00-carat diamond and a 0.90-carat diamond look the same size on the hand — but one can cost noticeably more. Here's why, and how to use it to your advantage.

~4 min read · Updated November 2026

One of the easiest ways to save money on a diamond is also one of the least known: buy just under a round carat weight. The size difference is invisible, but the price difference is real — and it's caused by nothing more than psychology.

"Magic weights" and why they cost more

Diamond prices don't rise smoothly as carat weight increases. They jump at certain round numbers — 0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.50, and 2.00 carats — known in the trade as "magic weights." Because so many buyers search for a nice round "1 carat," demand spikes at exactly those weights, and so does price. A diamond that tips just over 1.00ct can command a premium over one that lands at 0.90–0.95ct, even though they're nearly identical stones.

The trick: target weights just below a magic number — a 0.90–0.95ct instead of 1.00ct, or a 1.90ct instead of 2.00ct. You get virtually the same look for meaningfully less.

The size difference is tiny

Carat is a measure of weight, not size — and weight scales faster than the face-up diameter you actually see. The result: dropping from 1.00ct to 0.90ct changes the top diameter of a round diamond by only a few tenths of a millimeter.

WeightApprox. round diameterVisible difference
1.00 ct~6.5 mm—
0.90 ct~6.3 mmEssentially none

About two tenths of a millimeter — thinner than a stack of two sheets of paper — is not something anyone will notice on a finger. But the price can differ by double digits in percentage terms.

How much you save

The exact gap depends on the stone, but buying just under a magic weight commonly saves roughly 10–20% versus the round number. On a stone that would otherwise cross into "1 carat" pricing, that can be hundreds of dollars — for a diamond that looks the same. The savings tend to be even more pronounced at the 1.00ct threshold, which is the most sought-after magic weight of all.

How to shop for it

  • Search a range, not a round number. Instead of filtering for exactly 1.00ct, look at 0.90–0.99ct. You'll surface stones most buyers skip right past.
  • Compare price per carat, not just total price, to see the magic-weight jump clearly.
