GIA vs. IGI: Which Certificate Can You Trust?

The two names you'll see most on a diamond report — and the difference between them can quietly cost you money. Here's what each lab is good at, and how to compare grades fairly.

~5 min read · Updated November 2026

Every diamond worth buying comes with a grading report from an independent lab. Two names dominate: GIA (the Gemological Institute of America) and IGI (the International Gemological Institute). They both grade the same 4Cs — cut, color, clarity, and carat — but they don't grade identically, and knowing the difference helps you avoid overpaying for a grade that isn't quite what it seems.

GIA: the strictest grader

GIA is widely regarded as the strictest and most consistent grading laboratory in the world. When a GIA report says a diamond is G color and VS1 clarity, you can be confident it really is G, VS1. That consistency is why GIA is considered the gold standard, especially for natural diamonds.

The trade-off is that GIA's strictness can make a stone look slightly "worse" on paper than the same stone graded elsewhere — not because it's a lesser diamond, but because the yardstick is tougher.

IGI: the lab-grown standard

IGI grades the majority of the world's lab-grown diamonds — by most estimates, roughly two-thirds or more — and its reports are widely accepted by retailers and buyers. Historically, IGI has graded a touch more generously than GIA, so the same diamond can appear a half-grade or so better on an IGI report.

That doesn't make IGI "wrong" — it's a respected, globally recognized lab. It simply means an IGI grade and a GIA grade aren't perfectly interchangeable.

Key 2026 update: As of October 2025, GIA stopped issuing its traditional 4C grades for lab-grown diamonds, replacing them with a simpler two-tier quality assessment. In practice, that means nearly every lab-grown diamond you shop today will carry an IGI report — and that's completely normal, not a red flag.

The one rule that matters: compare like with like

The single most useful takeaway: don't pit a GIA grade against an IGI grade one-to-one. If you're weighing two natural diamonds, compare GIA to GIA. If you're weighing two lab-grown diamonds, compare IGI to IGI. Comparing across labs is where people get tricked into thinking one stone is a better deal than it really is.

And whichever lab issued the report, verify the report number on the lab's own website (gia.edu or igi.org) to confirm the diamond matches its paperwork. Our guide to reading a certificate walks through exactly what to check.

Does the lab change the price?

It can. Two diamonds with identical-looking specs may be priced differently partly because of which lab graded them, and partly because of the tiny grading differences above. That's exactly why it pays to compare the actual price of real stones at the specs you want, rather than trusting a grade in isolation.
